# Codebase audit: preservation admission freshness

## Confirmed defect

Manual crafting requires all selected raw-food lots to be fresh at admission, but trusted-load crafting replay only reconstructed batch material, temperature, outputs, resources, and work schedule. A persisted in-flight preservation job could report input exposure at or beyond the raw shelf-life limit and still load. The generic production history validator checks representability and that the checkpoint is anchored to admission; neither establishes food eligibility. This permitted otherwise prohibited conversion into long-lived edible provisions.

## Correction

The crafting replay validator now uses the survival owner's `freshness_from_history` with the authored raw-food shelf life and the existing work-in-process storage history at `job.started_at()`. Production checkpoints the oldest selected input exposure, and the manual batch contract requires one commodity, so the oldest exposure is sufficient to replay the admission predicate. The result must be fresh. A typed `FoodInputFreshnessInvalid` diagnostic identifies the job and commodity. This does not re-check freshness at the current tick: properly admitted work can cross the raw shelf-life boundary while its matter is held in production, and completion still retains the full accumulated age.

No persisted fields, schema, alternate freshness arithmetic, runtime admission semantics, or powered-craft policy changed. The existing food test and new persistence test live together in `src/crafting/personal_tests/food.rs`, with focused imports, rather than lengthening the general personal-crafting test module. Current behavior is documented in `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`.

## Verification

- The new regression failed on the unfixed core at the assertion that an already-spoiled pending nut job must be rejected. Its first build had unused must-use tick receipts; these were corrected before observing the intended failure. The fixed test covers both nut sorting and tuber curing, with one tick of raw shelf life left at admission and a save at expiry. Altering only admission exposure to the exact spoilage threshold is rejected with the typed error. Untampered saves continue identically to the original state, yielding 450 g nuts or 400 g tubers from 500 g input, retaining full exposure age and conserved matter. Tuber rejection was not separately observed failing on baseline because the shared regression stopped at the nut case.
- Actual text-play replay used ordinary gathering on seed 24318 to obtain berries and 500 g nuts. To isolate the boundary without asserting ten days of ordinary survival, a temporary checkpoint advanced the clock to tick 239999 and removed the opening colony; the raw nut storage history remained unchanged. The old executable accepted a forged pending job with 240000000000 ambient-age parts and completed it, returning 450 g sorted shell-on nuts and 50 g fragments. An exploratory script's final assertion used an incorrect display name, although the completion receipts were observed. The final executable check used the actual commodity label.
- Rebuilt text-play in the user's project. At tick 240000 the ordinary craft command rejected spoiled nuts. A legitimate job admitted at tick 239999 and saved at tick 240000 had 239999000000 parts at admission; the altered checkpoint was rejected with exit 2 and the freshness diagnostic. The unmodified save loaded and completed, yielding the exact two output parcels; the carried sorted nuts were still fresh. All temporary slots were removed.
- `python ci.py quick`: four checks passed. Focused crafting suite: 86 passed. Final `python ci.py audit --all`: 2,860 core and 187 gameplay tests passed (workshop scope, controlled-capability variation `0x977DD33CFB6D4FB8`). Task diff and `git diff --check` reviewed; the source-file line-ending warning on the reorganized test module was not a diff error.

## Scope and limits

Reviewed inventory selection, storage exposure, coalescing, ingress, production reservation and completion, job schedule and suspension replay, manual labor resumption, maintenance material exchange, equipment occupancy and embodiment. The specific confirmed inconsistency was at the manual food preservation trusted-load boundary. The check validates recorded eligibility, not an independently reconstructible per-lot history or authenticated save provenance. No powered food-preservation recipe is authored and no new policy was imposed on powered transforms. The boundary fixture advances the clock and isolates residents; it is not a full ordinary-play survival run. No performance improvement or exhaustive defect-free status is claimed. All twelve pre-existing terrain/custody changes are excluded; verification ran on the combined working tree, not an isolated clean checkout.

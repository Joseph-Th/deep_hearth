# Text Play

**Role:** Headless player interface and agent-operated gameplay review.

Deep Hearth has a terminal play client over the **production** world, player control, simulation, observation, and persistence paths. It is for playing, discovering friction, and tracing consequences. It does not replace mechanical contracts or invent alternate gameplay rules.

The client separates session setup and save/load (`src/bin/text_play/mod.rs`), command and direction parsing (`command.rs`), canonical control stepping and typed event reporting (`stepping.rs`, `stepping/feedback.rs`, and `stepping/feedback/events.rs`), and read-only presentation (`view.rs`, `view/sensory.rs`, and `activity.rs`). Direct action receipts, physical outcomes, locally observed scout encounters, weather, sounds, and scents use player-facing descriptions instead of raw debug records; local change detection compares typed observations, including ambient temperature changes independent of wind, rather than formatted strings. Same-frame pickups retain their source commodity in the authoritative receipt even when a later action removes the incoming lot, and feedback follows the physical control order. Storage and fire construction blockers report named materials and exact shortages; Celsius readouts retain fractions when a safety boundary is close. Active gathering labels obtain the player's reserved material through the surface owner's read-only projection, rather than using the available-action catalog as a second source of truth.

Gathering defaults are suggested portions, not maximum batches. Explicit amounts in single or repeated gathering use the authored method ceiling, available source and carrying capacity. Repetition and recovery after automatic pickup refresh the exact source and method through the surface owner; each new batch still requires normal admission.

Daylight watches compare only locally visible residents while continuing to settle every control tick. They do not repeatedly survey unrelated water, forage, scent, weather or track read models; survival and event interruptions still use their own authoritative observations.

## Launch

From the Deep Hearth directory:

~~~text
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --seed 24318
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --seed 24318 --script path/to/commands.txt
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --random
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --random --habitat dry
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --natural --seed 1837596006 --habitat damp
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --natural --seed 1837596006 --script path/to/commands.txt
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --load campaign_01
~~~

**World variety and reproducibility:** `--random` takes an unpredictable seed from operating-system-seeded randomness, prints the seed, and creates an unguided natural wilderness. `--natural --seed NUMBER` recreates that exact world, position, generated resources, and subsequent environmental changes for the same player commands. `--natural` without a seed also selects an unpredictable seed. `--habitat dry|mixed|damp` on either natural start deterministically selects a physically occupiable site from the actual seed-derived dry scrub, mixed woodland, or damp grove. Spawn search checks each safe cell's actual habitat within a sampled district before discarding the district, rather than accepting only its first safe cell. It prefers a physically admissible site with a two-step level walking route within the initially generated chunk, falling back to a single level exit only where no longer route is available; this does not guarantee supplies or a route through other chunks. Selection occurs only during world creation and never repaints terrain, creates a guaranteed resource, or reveals remote habitat to an admitted player. Seed-specific starts vary in position, 64 m regional hills and valleys over finer roughness, local weather, woodland, wild food, finite springs, and proximity to ancient sites. In the underlying geology, 128 m provinces favor iron oxides, tin-bearing rock, sulfide minerals, or mineral-poor basement; each 32 m district has **at most one** four-block-deep shallow outcrop, with much lower discovery odds in poor regions. Deep mineral lenses also follow the regional rock type. A positive surface find is real finite ore, not a promise of metal or an invisible deposit marker. Natural 32 m districts additionally expose finite, seed-chosen loose stone, flint, clay, or fallen timber: drier ground favors stone and flint, wetter woodland favors clay and deadwood. In addition to those sparse mixed districts, suitable chunks contain a finite parcel of weathered stone beside real exposed rock with a level approach, and seed-selected trees may have adjacent fallen deadwood. The initial natural chunk and streamed chunks use the same admission path; no stock responds to player needs. Parcels are genuinely gatherable, can be depleted, and persist through saved exploration. Already generated chunks are not repopulated. The original terrain elevations are retained in a bounded 32 m central reference clearing for regression-command compatibility; a smooth transition leads into more varied land beyond it. Randomly selected natural starts are not tied to that clearing. Unassisted natural worlds may still have no food, water, useful raw material, copper deposit, or opening kobold colony immediately at spawn; these are exploration scenarios, **not** a guaranteed end-to-end technology bootstrap. The current generated-world owners still limit terrain and ecology complexity.

**Biological diversity:** Damp groves contain alder and willow, seasonal berries and occasional edible woodland fungi. Mixed woodlands support oak and birch, berries/nuts and seed-varying tuber or fungus patches. Dry scrub has pinyon pine, juniper, wild nuts and buried starchy tubers. Young pioneer trees occur across habitats. Species accumulate real wood at different rates: willow grows fastest, alder and birch are also fast, oak grows more slowly, and dry-land pinyon and juniper mature much more slowly. This changes future canopy sizes and physical felling yields without changing the conserved wood budget. Real crown widths affect shade; pinyon and juniper retain some winter shade while deciduous species drop their leaves. In daylight, `look` identifies nearby trees through real open trunk faces and more distant woodland landmarks within a 12-cell horizontal search square using an occlusion-tested voxel ray. Unloaded terrain, stone, other trunks, and darkness block the longer sightline. A sighting does not extend chopping range: equip a woodworking adze, approach the trunk, and use `break` to fell its finite timber. Edible fungi offer hydration but spoil rapidly; tubers take longer to dig and keep longer. Tubers are one-time finite roots. Fungal colonies have a separate finite mycelium reserve: first mushrooms return to that reserve when they wither, and further spring/autumn crops can spend it under cool, suitable conditions. Shade and retained natural spring moisture improve fungal fruiting, while shrubs need daylight and mainly fruit in spring/summer. Regrowth conserves material, harvesting depletes the reserve, and discovering a distant site late cannot refresh its first mushrooms. Habitats are not yet animal ecosystems; there is no wildlife population simulation, biological reproduction or full soil ecology.

Without `--natural` or `--random`, `--seed NUMBER` continues to use the controlled seven-position opening with physically placed berries, nuts, water, timber, stone, clay/flint and a stone-era copper ore deposit. The default seed remains 24318. This maintained starter exists for exact command-script regression, not as evidence of how diverse the natural world is. Both starts admit the same ordinary player, use the same terrain/chunk/biome/resource/simulation owners, and save/load normally. Starting mode is not a persistent gameplay flag: after saving, use `--load SLOT`. World-source arguments are mutually exclusive with `--load`; `--random` cannot be combined with `--seed` or `--natural`. A seed is not secret world knowledge: it is a reproducibility identifier, while in-world observation stays restricted to the player's actual view. `status` and `look` identify the local habitat, not the coordinates of undiscovered resources.

## Commands

- **look [direction]**: condition, visible resources, tree species, water, kobolds, locally materialized framing, smells and sounds. The compact nearby-footing readout uses real generated terrain, standing-body clearance and grounding to distinguish level steps, nearby downward landings, possible one-block climbs needing a jump, and blocked or steep routes; it does not promise a traversable path beyond one step. A level destination with a blocked standing-body sweep at the current perpendicular offset is labeled **level (approach obstructed)**. Sightings include relative offsets, not pathfinding. Distant visible water and surface materials are reported within a 12-cell horizontal search square and six cells of elevation, only along a clear illuminated eye ray through generated terrain. These observations do not grant remote drinking or gathering, reveal quantities or create new supplies. Repeated identical kobold tracks are grouped with an impression count; distinct positions, headings and activities remain visible. Hunger, thirst, and unheated comfort runways use elapsed time; warnings list only active conditions. The unheated comfort estimate assumes resting exposure without external or exertion heat. Active physical work now spends its usual metabolic energy and offsets a bounded share of cold loss; severe exposure still cools the player. The unheated estimate assumes current exposure and reports the next weather-change boundary when that boundary precedes or coincides with the projected cold warning. The standing-space hint beneath a prospective roof uses the logistics owner's full-body collision and grounding rules, including living trunks, built floors, and occupied cells; it does not promise a reachable path. Optional directions use the bounded player-eye block ray: **n**, **s**, **e**, **w**, **up**, **down**, **n-down**, **s-down**, **e-down**, **w-down** (steeply downward to reach neighboring floor faces), and **n-shallow**, **s-shallow**, **e-shallow**, **w-shallow** (shallow downward angle to target the top of a neighboring timber post), and **n-up**, **s-up**, **e-up**, **w-up** (upward angle to target elevated framing or panels). Angled directions aim; they are not movement commands. **look at X Y Z**, **use at X Y Z**, and **break at X Y Z** aim the real eye ray at a voxel center, retaining the normal reach and first-hit restrictions. Pointed **look** also previews any structure that **break** would select ahead of the terrain block, so dismantling a roof or post is not hidden behind the ordinary stone-ray description.
- **status**, **inventory** (**inv**), **gather**, **drink**: inspect health, Celsius weather, selected hand, stance/foot elevation, motion, scheduled work, carried equipment, numbered physical stacks and exact-local drinkable water sources without spending time. Inventory marks physically mixed stacks without revealing a hidden exact ore assay, and lists the survival-owned spoilage estimate for each distinct food cohort in carried inventory and local storage. Inventory, gathering quantities and work labels retain fractional grams down to milligram precision; drinking volumes retain fractional milliliters. Consumed selected materials display '(none carried)' until the quick slot is rebound or cleared. Survival warnings use authored thresholds; injured players see vitality to two decimal places and a conditional time to full recovery at unchanged dietary balance, or the reason recovery is paused. The estimate assumes adequate food, water and warmth without further injury; a radiating fire at the player's position or in a face-adjacent cell reports its finite warmth, position and the estimated passive burn time remaining, using the energy owner's dissipation projection. The estimate assumes no additional fuel or other energy transfers. Nearby heat does not grant access to fueling or other exact-local actions.
- **n**, **s**, **e**, **w** optionally with control ticks: walk via the real continuous body controller. **sprint e [ticks]** and **sneak e [ticks]** apply the authored sprint and crouch-walk modes, with any cardinal direction. **crouch [ticks]** crouches in place; **jump** or **jump e [ticks]** applies one genuine vertical impulse, optionally followed by continuous horizontal motion. Movement is bounded to 240 ticks and stops on the first *horizontal* voxel crossing, important event, or confirmed stall, releasing the held input; pushing against an impassable obstacle, including after a failed jump, does not consume the full requested interval. An upward voxel crossing by itself does not stop a jump. A directional jump is not cut short by ordinary nearby-sighting changes while still crossing a ledge, although physical impacts, explicit simulation events and new survival warnings still stop it. Directional input during a jump can rebuild horizontal velocity after the rising body clears a ledge; neutral airborne input retains momentum, and no additional jump impulse is applied. The authoritative body still has no automatic step-up or climbing; a timed directional jump can cross a one-voxel ledge if its physical trajectory clears it, and other terrain may require finding another route. One tick is 50 ms. **center** uses bounded ordinary movement input to settle within 3 cm of the current cell center when standing space is clear; it requires settled movement and no active work, and can stop on obstruction or an event. The status readout reports horizontal offsets within the cell. Centering does not teleport.
- **trek DIRECTION [cells]**: deliberate cardinal travel over one to 32 cells (default eight). Each leg targets the center of the next real level, one-meter rise or one-meter descent foothold, using bounded ordinary control samples, a single jump for a climb and reduced horizontal speed for descending. It requires settled movement and no active work. It stops on deeper drops, missing clearance, failed approaches, physical events other than harmless landings, changed survival conditions, changed nearby resources/water, new close or defensive residents or newly visible distant resources. It does not pathfind around a cliff, teleport, change fall rules, or guarantee a physically successful approach. Raw controls remain available.
- **wait [ticks]**, **wait work**, **wait finish**, or **wait boundary**: neutral 20 Hz stepping. Work waits use the actual scheduled completion (including mining), capped at 7200 ticks per command. Ordinary waits report changing nearby creatures, resources, sounds, scents, and weather. **wait work** ignores routine patrol movement, footsteps, footprints, scent drift, and weather, but stops for newly close or defensive residents, changing physical resources or water, actual events, and new survival warnings. **wait finish** follows the same work deadline and interruption rules with a 72,000-tick (one simulated hour) cap. Either work wait may need repeating when its cap or an interruption is reached. **wait until morning**, **wait until afternoon**, **wait until evening**, or **wait until night** watches for the next requested daylight phase using the real control clock, without sleeping or accelerating survival. Long watches ignore routine patrol motion, footsteps, footprints, and wind shifts, but stop for an additional close resident (including one on an adjacent ledge or of an already visible caste), a newly defensive resident, real gameplay events, new survival warnings, or loss of heat from any fire warming the player when the watch began, even if other fires remain lit. They refuse to start with hunger, thirst, cold exposure, death or active work; stable existing injury does not prevent a watch, and changed survival conditions interrupt it. If already in the requested phase, no time passes. **wait boundary** advances to the next 72-control-tick slow-simulation boundary, or does nothing when already aligned; prospecting and manual work can only begin at that boundary. A rejected work start at a non-boundary tick explicitly recommends this command. Default wait is 72 ticks (3.6 seconds); **wait 10** settles walking momentum.
- **gather 1**, **gather 1 100**, or **gather 1 0.125**: attempt a listed exact-local resource, using its numbered row and optionally decimal grams with up to three fractional digits (exact milligrams). The displayed default is bounded by unreserved carrying capacity, so nearly full packs suggest smaller batches and full packs report no room. Partial resource remnants remain selectable instead of being rounded down to zero by the text interface. Physical availability, ongoing work, and storage admission are validated by production. If an automatic pickup changes the surface revision during the input frame, text play refreshes and retries the **same resource and method**, with bounded ordinary control ticks; it never disables stale-state rejection or silently switches to another row. **gather 1 repeat 8** or **gather 1 1000 repeat 8** repeats up to 32 ordinary, individually timed batches from the same original resource; each batch rechecks the currently observed source and exact physical capacity. Repetition stops when a source disappears, the batch no longer fits, or a work wait is interrupted; it never switches to another numbered resource. Only the matching physical completion receipt counts as a completed batch.
- **eat STACK**: eat from one numbered carried physical food stack shown by **inventory**. A different freshness or temperature cohort can be chosen even when both stacks are the same commodity. The command revalidates the exact inventory snapshot before consumption. If the next control frame settles a slow tick, it first advances that frame neutrally and refreshes the same physical cohort. A changed inventory revision or cohort is rejected rather than silently retargeting another row. Intake receipts show physical quantities and completion timing without private material lot identities or internal accounting fields.
- **drink SOURCE**: drink from one numbered safe, exact-local fluid source listed by **drink**. Multiple springs and co-located gatherable material no longer prevent an explicit drinking choice. Only current local access, actual hydration need, temperature, finite fluid volume and player attention authorize the action.
- **recipes**, **recipes all**: show carried-input manual recipe choices or the complete authored recipe catalog. Rows use stable process IDs, current compatible input counts, tool requirements, and conserved products. Catalog visibility does not authorize work.
- **craft PROCESS_ID [batches] [from STACK] [with TOOL]**: start a manual recipe through production-backed personal crafting. Default is one batch. Freshness-sensitive inputs require a numbered carried stack (**from 1**); optional or required tools use numbered carried equipment (**with 1**) from **inventory**. Admission rejects invalid requests without mutation. Completion deposits exact physical outputs.
- **surveys [width]**, **prospect MATERIAL METHOD [width] [with TOOL]**, **knowledge [width]**: inspect authored field-survey methods, perform a real timed survey for a named material (such as **copper**) or an authored material ID, and read only acquired geological evidence. The bounded region starts at the player's voxel and extends **width** voxels east and south at the current height; width is 1–4. Survey methods have distinct precision, duration, and required equipment. Tool numbers reference carried equipment in **inventory**. **knowledge** shows measured abundance ranges, hardness where sampled, and historical resource-mass ranges, never hidden live reserves. For wide regions and multi-observation localities it also lists acquired sample footprints, estimates, and measurement times, distinguishing disjoint samples from contradictory measurements. Aggregate 2×2 evidence cannot be claimed as one exact-voxel survey.
- **mine MATERIAL GRAMS with TOOL [using METHOD]**: use acquired exact-local evidence to authorize a finite mining batch with a carried tool and output into carried inventory. GRAMS accepts decimal grams to 0.001 g, matching finite geology and inventory precision. Defaults to method 1 (hand pick); authored method 2 is hand digging. Method, tool hardness, equipment condition, batch limit, output capacity, and work duration remain production-validated. An ordinary copper-reinforced pick has a 300 g maximum per batch. Mining a target after a coarse survey does not require separate hardness evidence if the equipment can physically work it. Append **repeat COUNT** (1–32) to perform ordinary sequential batches on the original target with the original tool. Each batch revalidates admission and waits for its matching output receipt; depletion, insufficient capacity, wear-related rejection or interruption ends repetition.
- **ore**, **dress PROCESS STACK GRAMS**: show authored hand ore operations and attempt a single selected carried-material batch using real process, particle-size, composition, custody, and survival rules. GRAMS accepts decimal grams to 0.001 g, so subgram ore remnants can still be processed when physically valid. **dress 15 STACK 100** breaks up to 100 g of ore into coarse sortable fragments; **dress 13 STACK 100** sorts up to 200 g of suitably crushed ore for native copper and physical residue. Both require time and place all outputs in the carried inventory; they do not discard gangue or use hidden machinery. The initial command works by hand, not with an optional tool; tool-assisted ore handling and broader process-site routing remain outside this text adapter.
- **gear** and **gear all**: list currently material-backed equipment assembly choices and the full authored assembly catalog, with exact missing-material requirements and local stationary gear. **assemble DEFINITION_ID** consumes carried parts and constructs a stationary tool. **take N** picks up a local tool; **equip N** holds the numbered carried tool; **recover N** disassembles a local stationary tool into its recoverable parts. Material constraints, location, capacity, condition, and attention remain production-owned.
- **storage**, **storage all**, **place-store DEFINITION_ID**: inspect placeable storage, inspect all authored storage and blockers, and construct an enclosure at the player's actual grounded voxel from carried parts. **stash CONTAINER STACK [grams]** and **withdraw CONTAINER STACK [grams]** move the maximum physically fitting amount by default, or the explicit exact amount (decimal grams to 0.001 g) when supplied, between a numbered local container and a numbered material stack, under canonical access/capacity checks. An explicit amount that exceeds available mass or capacity is rejected rather than silently truncated. Inventory lists both sides; remote containers cannot be accessed.
- **fire**, **build-fire**, **feed-fire FIRE STACK**: inspect local campfires, fuel choices, and eligible carried stack indices; construct a stone-ring fire from 1 kg of carried stone; or feed a local fire from a numbered carried stack. Combustion consumes only the selected physical stack, never another same-commodity cohort; pure remnants smaller than a full charge burn with proportionate heat, while impure stacks are rejected. Check **fire** after inventory changes because stack indices can shift; each eligible charge shows its exact selected mass and thermal energy before commitment. Full charges consume 1 kg of timber log (12 MJ), 500 g of wood chips (5 MJ), or 500 g of shaped timber board (6 MJ); smaller selected stacks burn their available mass and release scaled, rounded-down nanojoules. A source chosen only by commodity or by the contextual **use** action still requires a full charge. When the fire lacks capacity, its blocker displays stored, maximum, and attempted charge energy in MJ. Dismantled timber boards remain usable firewood. Fuel and energy remain finite, and warmth depends on the real local lit fire.
- **use [direction]**: contextual secondary action. An empty hand may gather or drink when the local context allows it; use **drink N** to choose a specific co-located source instead. Selected food is eaten even while at water; use **empty** first to drink contextually, or **eat N** to choose a specific food cohort. Selected logs can form timber framing when aimed at a neighboring floor with **use e-down**; selected boards need an authored supported structural target, such as the top of a finished nearby post targeted with **use e-shallow**. With boards selected, **look e-shallow** previews the same physical panel target as construction and reports whether a grounded standing-height space exists beneath it, before any material is committed. The preview uses the world's full-cell collision classification, so a standing tree is not mistaken for empty shelter space and non-supporting terrain is not accepted as footing. A potential space does not guarantee a reachable walking route. Stand outside the proposed cover voxel; panels reject intersecting bodies and occupied foliage. These start real timed work and may buffer until the next slow tick. A supported panel is not automatically a usable shelter: the player must fit beneath the active roof's footprint with enough head clearance, and **look** reports nearby panels that fail to cover the current standing position. To extend cover from raised framing or a cap panel over lower ground, approach from the lower side, use **look s-up** (or the matching cardinal-up direction), and check for a potential standing space before using the same aim to build. The full body footprint must lie beneath overhead cover; **center** helps position the body inside the covered cell. Selected materials also support other authored placement/combustion.
- **plan-at STACK X Y Z**, **build-at STACK X Y Z**: preview or begin supported timber construction from a specific carried log or board stack at an exact destination. At a settled slow boundary, the preview validates current work admission, materials, destination reach, unobstructed eye ray, occupancy, and active face-adjacent structural supports without mutation. Build uses the same validation and ordinary timed construction; no member provides cover until completed. The first grounded post still uses the contextual floor-face **use** action. Extend that post upward and then attach a panel horizontally above the player, without needing a hillside.
- **break**, **shove**, **strike**, **prepare**, **release** with optional direction; **cancel**: canonical pointed primary actions. **look DIRECTION** identifies a structural break target when one takes priority over terrain; completed structural dismantling reports the actual recovered mass entering the player's carried pack.
- **slot 1** through **slot 9**, **select 1**, **empty**, **drop**: switch quick slots, select a numbered carried stack's commodity/form into the active slot, clear selection, or drop the active material. Hotbar selection retains the commodity/form, not an individual lot's freshness or temperature; use **eat STACK**, **craft ... from STACK**, **dress**, **feed-fire**, or **stash/withdraw** for exact physical stack choices.
- **respawn**: after death, use the real validated respawn path. The new carried pack is empty; the old pack and dropped tools remain recoverable in a death cache at the reported location. Attempting this while alive is rejected without changing state.
- **save campaign_01**, **quit**: save the current validated world to a *new* slot or end play. A save is not automatically created when quitting.

An input file passed to --script has one command per line. Blank and # comment lines are ignored, and the transcript echoes each executed command. An invalid command or failed command admission stops the script at its numbered line with a nonzero exit status; later commands do not execute. Canonical in-world action rejections returned as typed outcomes remain visible events rather than script errors. Work may require further waits, and unrelated visible events can interrupt waiting early.

An ordinary flint-crafting example, on seed 24318, is: **e 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10**, **gather 3 300**, **wait work**, **recipes**, **craft 99**, **wait work**, **inventory**. It consumes 300 g of flint nodule and produces 220 g of knapped flint cutting head and 80 g of flakes.

To make and use a flint woodworking knife from the same seed: move to the timber log at (10,2,9) with **e 20**, **wait 10**, **e 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10**; use **gather 1 1000**, **wait work**, **craft 8**, **wait work** for the handle. Move **w 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10** to flint; use **gather 3 300**, **wait work**, **craft 99**, **wait work**, **gear**, **assemble 58**, **take 1**, **equip 1**. The assembled 420 g knife can replace hand timing in compatible recipes such as **craft 14 with 1**, with actual condition wear.

To make a 10 kg field box from timber: gather 2 kg at (10,2,9), use **craft 14 2**, **wait work**, **craft 27**, **wait work**, then move to the clear (9,2,10) site with **w 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10**. **storage** shows whether the site is placeable; **place-store 5** creates the enclosure. **stash 1 1** puts its first carried stack into the box; **withdraw 1 1** retrieves its first local stack. The timber-source site is resident-occupied and correctly rejects placement.

For a campfire: from (8,2,8) go east three voxel crossings, braking with **wait 10** after each, then **gather 1 1000**, **wait work**, **build-fire** at the stone source. Retrieve 1 kg of timber from the (10,2,9) source and return to the fire, then **fire**, **feed-fire 1 1**, **status**. A lit fire consumes finite fuel and can restore cold-weather comfort. For a timber post, gather 3,250 g of log, select its inventory stack, **look e-down**, **use e-down**, then wait for the buffered construction and **wait work**. **look** shows nearby materialized framing.

For the first-copper path on seed 24318: **no hand-gatherable native copper appears in the opening**. Make stone tools first: gather **2 kg stone** at (11,2,8), use **craft 7 2**; gather **2 kg timber** at (10,2,9), use **craft 8 2**. Finish real work with **wait work**, then **assemble 21**, **take 1** for the geological hammer and **assemble 6**, **take 1** for the knapped stone pick. Walk to the clay/flint outcrop (9,2,10), use **wait boundary**, **prospect copper 1**, **wait work**; the coarse survey alone does not authorize mining the sparse ore. Perform **prospect copper 2 with 1**, **wait work**, **knowledge** to acquire a more precise deposit estimate and its stone-workable hardness. Use **mine copper 200 with 2**, **wait work** twice to obtain 400 g copper ore. In **ore**, find the numbered ore stack, use **dress 15 STACK 100**, **wait work** to break 100 g; then **dress 13 STACK 100**, **wait work** on the resulting crushed-ore stack to recover **6.5 g native copper** and retain 93.5 g of physical residue. Repeat breaking and sorting four 100 g ore portions to recover **26 g**. Only then can **craft 10**, **wait work** consume 20 g of native copper for one reinforcement. Copper equipment requires its other physical parts or upgrading a preexisting stone tool; no copper tool or ore is inserted into the starting inventory. Check **inventory** after every material transformation, as numbered stacks change. The 10%-copper deposit, finite ore, material residues, and work times are authoritative production outcomes.

## Save and scope

save NAME writes saves/NAME.sav as JSON through the current exact-schema SaveEnvelope. --load NAME validates the entire persisted state before the player resumes. Slot names are 1–48 ASCII alphanumeric, hyphens, or underscores. A slot never intentionally overwrites an existing save. The text client is responsible for local file IO; the core simulation remains headless and pure.

The text interface covers an **opening wilderness slice including manual crafting, tool assembly, acquired geological surveys, finite copper mining and hand ore dressing, local storage, campfire warmth, basic pointed framing and 20 Hz body movement**, not the full powered-workshop interface, industrial foundry progression, general building-plan UI, spatial rendering, or sound reproduction. It cannot judge first-person motion feel or visual quality. Its observation model is deliberately limited to existing actor-local projections, never direct access to hidden geology, future events, or all remote resources.

Use text play to find unexpected obstacles, confusion, questionable pacing, missed cues, and ordinary abandonment; then add focused owner tests for confirmed production defects. The automated harness exists for bounded system regression and comparison evidence: probes now execute fixed disclosed command scripts, and their reports describe measured physical behavior. A scripted probe is not a gameplay reviewer — deciding what a player would try next belongs to a real text-play session.

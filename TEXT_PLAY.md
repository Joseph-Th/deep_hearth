# Text Play

**Role:** Headless player interface and agent-operated gameplay review.

Deep Hearth has a terminal play client over the **production** world, player control, simulation, observation, and persistence paths. It is for playing, discovering friction, and tracing consequences. It does not replace mechanical contracts or invent alternate gameplay rules.

The client separates session setup and save/load (`src/bin/text_play/mod.rs`), command and direction parsing (`command.rs`), canonical control stepping and typed event reporting (`stepping.rs`, `stepping/feedback.rs`, and `stepping/feedback/events.rs`), and read-only presentation (`view.rs`, `view/sensory.rs`, and `activity.rs`). Direct action receipts, physical outcomes, locally observed scout encounters, weather, sounds, and scents use player-facing descriptions instead of raw debug records; local change detection compares typed observations rather than formatted strings. Same-frame pickups retain their source commodity in the authoritative receipt even when a later action removes the incoming lot, and feedback follows the physical control order. Storage and fire construction blockers report named materials and exact shortages; Celsius readouts retain fractions when a safety boundary is close. Active gathering labels obtain the player's reserved material through the surface owner's read-only projection, rather than using the available-action catalog as a second source of truth.

## Launch

From the Deep Hearth directory:

~~~text
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --seed 24318
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --seed 24318 --script path/to/commands.txt
cargo run --locked --bin text-play -- --load campaign_01
~~~

The default seed is 24318. Other seeds produce reproducible openings. The seeded opening uses canonical generated terrain and the authoritative finite wilderness trail, admits a normal survival player, and generates surrounding chunks on demand. The opening may include berries, nuts, water, timber, stone, and real kobold residents. Unobserved resources remain hidden.

## Commands

- **look [direction]**: condition, visible resources, water, kobolds, locally materialized framing, smells and sounds. Sightings include relative offsets, not pathfinding. Hunger, thirst, and unheated comfort runways use elapsed time; warnings list only active conditions. Optional directions use the bounded player-eye block ray: **n**, **s**, **e**, **w**, **up**, **down**, and **n-down**, **s-down**, **e-down**, **w-down** (angled downward to reach neighboring floor faces). Angled directions aim; they are not movement commands.
- **status**, **inventory** (**inv**), **gather**, **drink**: inspect health, Celsius weather, selected hand, stance/foot elevation, motion, scheduled work, carried equipment, numbered physical stacks and exact-local drinkable water sources without spending time. Inventory, gathering quantities and work labels retain fractional grams down to milligram precision; drinking volumes retain fractional milliliters. Consumed selected materials display '(none carried)' until the quick slot is rebound or cleared. Survival warnings use authored thresholds; an active local fire reports its finite stored warmth.
- **n**, **s**, **e**, **w** optionally with control ticks: walk via the real continuous body controller. **sprint e [ticks]** and **sneak e [ticks]** apply the authored sprint and crouch-walk modes, with any cardinal direction. **crouch [ticks]** crouches in place; **jump** or **jump e [ticks]** applies genuine vertical impulse, optionally with horizontal motion. Movement is bounded to 240 ticks and stops on the first *horizontal* voxel crossing or important event, releasing the held input; an upward voxel crossing by itself does not stop a jump. The authoritative body still has no automatic step-up or climbing, and terrain may require finding another route. One tick is 50 ms.
- **wait [ticks]**, **wait work**, or **wait boundary**: neutral 20 Hz stepping. Work waits use the actual scheduled completion (including mining), capped at 7200 ticks per command. Nearby creature, resource, sound, and scent changes are identified by type and can interrupt waits; weather-only changes do not interrupt **wait work**. Work outcomes and survival warning transitions always interrupt waits, so **wait work** may need repeating. **wait boundary** advances to the next 72-control-tick slow-simulation boundary, or does nothing when already aligned; prospecting and manual work can only begin at that boundary. A rejected work start at a non-boundary tick explicitly recommends this command. Default wait is 72 ticks (3.6 seconds); **wait 10** settles walking momentum.
- **gather 1**, **gather 1 100**, or **gather 1 0.125**: attempt a listed exact-local resource, using its numbered row and optionally decimal grams with up to three fractional digits (exact milligrams). The displayed default is bounded by unreserved carrying capacity, so nearly full packs suggest smaller batches and full packs report no room. Partial resource remnants remain selectable instead of being rounded down to zero by the text interface. Physical availability, ongoing work, and storage admission are validated by production.
- **eat STACK**: eat from one numbered carried physical food stack shown by **inventory**. A different freshness or temperature cohort can be chosen even when both stacks are the same commodity. The command makes one direct-control request and revalidates that exact inventory snapshot before any consumption; old choices cannot retarget after custody or aging changes. Intake receipts show physical quantities and completion timing without private material lot identities or internal accounting fields.
- **drink SOURCE**: drink from one numbered safe, exact-local fluid source listed by **drink**. Multiple springs and co-located gatherable material no longer prevent an explicit drinking choice. Only current local access, actual hydration need, temperature, finite fluid volume and player attention authorize the action.
- **recipes**, **recipes all**: show carried-input manual recipe choices or the complete authored recipe catalog. Rows use stable process IDs, current compatible input counts, tool requirements, and conserved products. Catalog visibility does not authorize work.
- **craft PROCESS_ID [batches] [from STACK] [with TOOL]**: start a manual recipe through production-backed personal crafting. Default is one batch. Freshness-sensitive inputs require a numbered carried stack (**from 1**); optional or required tools use numbered carried equipment (**with 1**) from **inventory**. Admission rejects invalid requests without mutation. Completion deposits exact physical outputs.
- **surveys [width]**, **prospect MATERIAL METHOD [width] [with TOOL]**, **knowledge [width]**: inspect authored field-survey methods, perform a real timed survey for a named material (such as **copper**) or an authored material ID, and read only acquired geological evidence. The bounded region starts at the player's voxel and extends **width** voxels east and south at the current height; width is 1–4. Survey methods have distinct precision, duration, and required equipment. Tool numbers reference carried equipment in **inventory**. **knowledge** shows measured abundance ranges, hardness where sampled, and historical resource-mass ranges, never hidden live reserves. For wide regions and multi-observation localities it also lists acquired sample footprints, estimates, and measurement times, distinguishing disjoint samples from contradictory measurements. Aggregate 2×2 evidence cannot be claimed as one exact-voxel survey.
- **mine MATERIAL GRAMS with TOOL [using METHOD]**: use acquired exact-local evidence to authorize a finite mining batch with a carried tool and output into carried inventory. Defaults to method 1 (hand pick); authored method 2 is hand digging. Method, tool hardness, equipment condition, batch limit, output capacity, and work duration remain production-validated. An ordinary copper-reinforced pick has a 300 g maximum per batch. Mining a target after a coarse survey does not require separate hardness evidence if the equipment can physically work it.
- **ore**, **dress PROCESS STACK GRAMS**: show authored hand ore operations and attempt a single selected carried-material batch using real process, particle-size, composition, custody, and survival rules. **dress 15 STACK 100** breaks up to 100 g of ore into coarse sortable fragments; **dress 13 STACK 100** sorts up to 200 g of suitably crushed ore for native copper and physical residue. Both require time and place all outputs in the carried inventory; they do not discard gangue or use hidden machinery. The initial command works by hand, not with an optional tool; tool-assisted ore handling and broader process-site routing remain outside this text adapter.
- **gear** and **gear all**: list currently material-backed equipment assembly choices and the full authored assembly catalog, with exact missing-material requirements and local stationary gear. **assemble DEFINITION_ID** consumes carried parts and constructs a stationary tool. **take N** picks up a local tool; **equip N** holds the numbered carried tool; **recover N** disassembles a local stationary tool into its recoverable parts. Material constraints, location, capacity, condition, and attention remain production-owned.
- **storage**, **storage all**, **place-store DEFINITION_ID**: inspect placeable storage, inspect all authored storage and blockers, and construct an enclosure at the player's actual grounded voxel from carried parts. **stash CONTAINER STACK** and **withdraw CONTAINER STACK** move the maximum physically fitting amount between a numbered local container and a numbered material stack, under canonical access/capacity checks. Inventory lists both sides; remote containers cannot be accessed.
- **fire**, **build-fire**, **feed-fire FIRE STACK**: inspect local campfires, fuel charges, and currently eligible carried fuel stack indices; construct a stone-ring fire from 1 kg of carried stone; or feed a local fire from a numbered carried stack. Check **fire** after inventory changes because stack indices can shift. One charge consumes 1 kg of timber log (12 MJ) or 500 g of wood chips (5 MJ). Fuel and energy remain finite, and warmth depends on the real local lit fire.
- **use [direction]**: contextual secondary action. An empty hand may gather or drink when the local context allows it; use **drink N** to choose a specific co-located source instead. Selected food is eaten even while at water; use **empty** first to drink contextually, or **eat N** to choose a specific food cohort. Selected logs can form timber framing when aimed at a neighboring floor with **use e-down**; selected boards need an authored supported structural target. These start real timed work and may buffer until the next slow tick. Selected materials also support other authored placement/combustion.
- **break**, **shove**, **strike**, **prepare**, **release** with optional direction; **cancel**: canonical pointed primary actions.
- **slot 1** through **slot 9**, **select 1**, **empty**, **drop**: quick bar, numbered carried stack binding, clear selection, and ordinary active drop.
- **respawn**: after death, use the real validated respawn path. The new carried pack is empty; the old pack and dropped tools remain recoverable in a death cache at the reported location. Attempting this while alive is rejected without changing state.
- **save campaign_01**, **quit**: save the current validated world to a *new* slot or end play. A save is not automatically created when quitting.

An input file passed to --script has one command per line. Blank and # comment lines are ignored, and the transcript echoes each executed command. An invalid command or failed command admission stops the script at its numbered line with a nonzero exit status; later commands do not execute. Canonical in-world action rejections returned as typed outcomes remain visible events rather than script errors. Work may require further waits, and unrelated visible events can interrupt waiting early.

An ordinary flint-crafting example, on seed 24318, is: **e 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10**, **gather 3 300**, **wait work**, **recipes**, **craft 99**, **wait work**, **inventory**. It consumes 300 g of flint nodule and produces 220 g of knapped flint cutting head and 80 g of flakes.

To make and use a flint woodworking knife from the same seed: move to the timber log at (10,2,9) with **e 20**, **wait 10**, **e 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10**; use **gather 1 1000**, **wait work**, **craft 8**, **wait work** for the handle. Move **w 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10** to flint; use **gather 3 300**, **wait work**, **craft 99**, **wait work**, **gear**, **assemble 58**, **take 1**, **equip 1**. The assembled 420 g knife can replace hand timing in compatible recipes such as **craft 14 with 1**, with actual condition wear.

To make a 10 kg field box from timber: gather 2 kg at (10,2,9), use **craft 14 2**, **wait work**, **craft 27**, **wait work**, then move to the clear (9,2,10) site with **w 20**, **wait 10**, **s 20**, **wait 10**. **storage** shows whether the site is placeable; **place-store 5** creates the enclosure. **stash 1 1** puts its first carried stack into the box; **withdraw 1 1** retrieves its first local stack. The timber-source site is resident-occupied and correctly rejects placement.

For a campfire: from (8,2,8) go east three voxel crossings, braking with **wait 10** after each, then **gather 1 1000**, **wait work**, **build-fire** at the stone source. Retrieve 1 kg of timber from the (10,2,9) source and return to the fire, then **fire**, **feed-fire 1 1**, **status**. A lit fire consumes finite fuel and can restore cold-weather comfort. For a timber post, gather 3,250 g of log, select its inventory stack, **look e-down**, **use e-down**, then wait for the buffered construction and **wait work**. **look** shows nearby materialized framing.

For the copper-opening path on seed 24318: gather **20 g native copper** at the clay outcrop (9,2,10) and use **craft 10** for reinforcement; gather **2 kg stone** at (11,2,8), use **craft 7 2** for two knapped stone-tool batches; gather **2 kg timber** at (10,2,9), use **craft 8 2** for handles. Finish each active work interval (repeating **wait work** after interruption), then **assemble 21**, **take 1** for the geological hammer and **assemble 8**, **take 1** for the reinforced pick. Return to (9,2,10); **wait boundary**, **prospect copper 1**, **wait work**, **prospect copper 2 with 1**, **wait work**, **knowledge** acquire physical evidence, including an outcrop hardness range. **mine copper 300 with 2**, **wait work** brings 300 g of copper ore into inventory. **ore** lists manual processing; **dress 15 5 100** breaks 100 g of the ore (check **inventory** for actual stack numbers); **dress 13 STACK 100** using the new crushed-ore stack recovers 32.5 g native copper from this 50%-copper seam while leaving the 67.5 g residue. **craft 10** can reuse 20 g of this recovered copper to produce more reinforcement. The remaining copper and physical gangue remain conserved.

## Save and scope

save NAME writes saves/NAME.sav as JSON through the current exact-schema SaveEnvelope. --load NAME validates the entire persisted state before the player resumes. Slot names are 1–48 ASCII alphanumeric, hyphens, or underscores. A slot never intentionally overwrites an existing save. The text client is responsible for local file IO; the core simulation remains headless and pure.

The text interface covers an **opening wilderness slice including manual crafting, tool assembly, acquired geological surveys, finite copper mining and hand ore dressing, local storage, campfire warmth, basic pointed framing and 20 Hz body movement**, not the full powered-workshop interface, industrial foundry progression, general building-plan UI, spatial rendering, or sound reproduction. It cannot judge first-person motion feel or visual quality. Its observation model is deliberately limited to existing actor-local projections, never direct access to hidden geology, future events, or all remote resources.

Use text play to find unexpected obstacles, confusion, questionable pacing, missed cues, and ordinary abandonment; then add focused owner tests for confirmed production defects. The automated harness exists for bounded system regression and comparison evidence: probes now execute fixed disclosed command scripts, and their reports describe measured physical behavior. A scripted probe is not a gameplay reviewer — deciding what a player would try next belongs to a real text-play session.

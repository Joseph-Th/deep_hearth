# Content inspiration and connected gaps

**Role:** Research and prioritization note for playable content. STATUS.md remains the
authority for implemented reachability, GAME_DESIGN.md for intent, and
GAMEPLAY_EVALUATION.md for the automated-player evidence boundary.

## What the references actually contribute

The [TerraFirmaCraft field guide's opening](https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Field-Guide/en_us/getting_started/introduction.html)
starts with loose rocks and sticks, knaps distinct heads, then assembles hand tools
whose verbs differ. [Its pottery guide](https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Field-Guide/en_us/getting_started/pottery.html)
connects clay vessels, molds, firing and later metal tools. The central lesson is
that an early material discovery should enable a useful *different action*, not
merely add a crafting recipe.

The [GregTech Modern steam-machinery guide](https://gregtechceu.github.io/GregTech-Modern/1.21/Gameplay/Steam/Steam-Usage/)
distinguishes machines by operation: maceration, compression, hammering, alloy
smelting and mining have different inputs and infrastructure constraints.
Mechanization saves tools and repetitive attention, then trades that advantage
for energy, site conditions, material throughput and equipment obligations.

These references inform Deep Hearth's own physical progression; their recipe
counts and names are not targets. Deep Hearth should favor concrete differences
in work, yield, access, preservation, risk and delegation over cosmetically
different tool tiers.

## Executed player evidence

The wilderness gameplay harness walks one generated opening from spawn to the
outcrop and back, collects finite flint and copper, carries stone and timber
to the junction, knaps a blade, forms the copper saw blade and chisel edge,
and assembles the three handheld tools through the ordinary player catalog.
It executes the flint knife against bare hewing, copper saw against hand
shaping, and copper chisel against unassisted crock carving from matched
post-assembly states. Each branch checks whole-world matter conservation and
strict loaded-state integrity. This is one **separate** generated-world experiment,
not a continuation of the earlier maintained wilderness/fire episode and not
evidence of a firing kiln, arbitrary regional terrain access or machine networks.

A subsequent **independent** generated-world episode returns to the clay outcrop,
hauls finite timber and stone, shapes a greenware crock and clay kiln lining,
builds the kiln and its fuel hearth from actual inventory, burns a one-kilogram
timber charge, and fires a ceramic body through the regular player-authorized
thermal job. The test rejects 900 K firing, accepts 1,100 K, checks in-flight
trusted-state validity, observes the conserved 1.6 kg fired body, and checks
the whole world's matter ledger and final load integrity. A complementary
owner test takes the body through real sensible cooling, then
storage-enclosure construction, checking the higher preservation multiplier,
the exact heat released to a finite stone bed and lossless transfer of the
vessel's mass into the enclosure. Firing heat is reserved from the hearth's
finite stored energy; its passive dissipation cannot be spent on a job.
The independent generated-world episode now also gathers the extra stone,
assembles the cooling bed, cools the 1,100 K ware to 293.15 K through a
separate player-authorized job, and verifies both live load-state integrity
and final conservation. The owner proof includes save/load replay mid-cooling,
and rejects a second cooling attempt once the ware is already at the target.
The fired 6 kg provisions crock needs only a 1.6 kg body and preserves
at 3.5x baseline shelf life; the carved-stone crock needs a 2.4 kg body
and preserves at 2.5x. This gives repeated kiln use an actual engineering
payoff rather than just a different-looking container at nearly the same
performance. The first kiln still demands more stone, timber and effort
than hand-carving, so it pays off as reusable local infrastructure.
The continued generated-world episode shapes an 800 g greenware ingot
mold, fires and cools it through the same kiln infrastructure, and
assembles reusable ceramic casting equipment. It installs the cooled
provisions crock at the worksite, physically revisits the forage patch,
and deposits 500 g of fresh berries in the sealed stockpile. Equipment
and enclosure construction now reject selected kiln-hot materials
without mutation. An independent owner test casts a real 20 g molten
copper batch using the ceramic mold: it runs faster than carved stone,
retains generated heat in a finite sink, and conserves copper mass.
Molten copper is supplied by a controlled fixture for this comparison,
so the episode does not prove ordinary electrical-furnace acquisition.
Ambient cooling, carried-hot-item injury, kiln chemistry and regional
clay acquisition remain unsupported.

## Highest-value remaining content slices

Complete greenware moisture and drying, firing soak and atmosphere,
passive ambient cooldown, hot-item injury and readable cracked rejects.
Then connect the ceramic casting mold to player-acquired molten copper
in one uninterrupted journey; follow with charcoal, alloys and heat
treatment under full chemical/thermal custody. Add powered machines only
where their manual counterparts and energy/logistics costs make
delegation worth the investment.
